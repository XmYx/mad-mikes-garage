# Mad Mike's Garage — Storyline proposal

**Working campaign title: KEEP THE LIGHT ON**

**Status:** narrative and gameplay proposal; no quests, characters, rewards or campaign rules below are claimed to be implemented. Names and balance values are provisional. This report builds on the current README, roadmap, factions, skills and vehicle systems. It proposes a separate STORY start alongside SANDBOX, preserving the latter's freedom.

> You wake under an overturned convoy trailer with a canteen, a bent wrench and a radio that only works when you hold its wires together. Somebody has taken the vehicles, the cargo and everyone you travelled with. At sunset, a familiar voice comes through the static: “If you made it, keep the light on.” Three towns away, a freight ledger says you died yesterday.

## 1. What the campaign should make the player want

The immediate question is **“Who took my people, and why am I listed as dead?”** The growing ambition is **“Can I build somewhere worth coming back to?”** The final question is **“Who gets to decide which settlements keep running?”**

The hero is an ordinary convoy mechanic/courier, with player-created appearance, attributes, traits and name. Their value comes from understanding broken things and choosing what to repair. They are not a chosen bloodline or the only person capable of saving the world. At character creation, choose whether missing convoy leader **Mara Vale** was a mentor, an old friend or a former employer who gave the hero a second chance. This changes personal dialogue, not access to an ending.

“Mad Mike's Garage” is the hand-painted name on a derelict roadside workshop. Mike is its absent former owner, not a forced player identity. The hero gradually makes the place their own, can keep or replace the sign, and can establish a different home without losing campaign functionality.

The story should reward curiosity with three things together: a useful change to the player's life, a memorable human encounter, and a new question. Recovering a pump makes clean water available **and** reveals the settlement whose allocation was stolen. Repairing a receiver produces music **and** a voice that should not be there. The world remains dangerous, funny and worn down, but people still argue about recipes, plant flowers and make terrible birthday presents.

### Narrative promises

- **A car becomes yours through use.** The first car is inadequate but dependable once repaired; scratches, repairs and optional keepsakes tell its history. Never confiscate it in a cutscene to force an upgrade.
- **Progress is visible.** Working lights, occupied seats, changed shop stock, patched roads and new radio callers matter more than an XP banner alone.
- **Answers arrive regularly.** Reveal who falsified the death record before the midpoint. The mystery then becomes what they were concealing and what the hero will do about it; do not defer every answer to the finale.
- **Urgency is honest.** No global countdown while the player is encouraged to fish. Timed rescues begin only after explicit acceptance and show their stakes. Main-story preparation can wait.
- **Violence is possible, not the universal verb.** Critical obstacles support a practical, negotiated or combat route. Low Speech changes the cost of a bargain; it does not invalidate a nonviolent campaign.
- **Rest is part of the story.** After a demanding chapter, leave room for a meal, an upgrade, a conversation or an unrelated outing before another crisis.

## 2. Story mode: start with almost nothing, without becoming a chore

| Starting provision | Proposed story-mode rule | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing | Worn everyday layers appropriate to the starting climate; no armour | Vulnerability without an unavoidable temperature death |
| Inventory | Bent but usable wrench, pocket knife, half-full canteen, one food portion, one clean bandage | Enough for the opening, little to sell |
| Money | Zero scrap currency | First earned payment feels meaningful |
| Transport | No owned working vehicle; one recoverable small car nearby | Establish repair and ownership as the first achievement |
| Knowledge | Basic survival and repair recipes; chosen traits still apply | Respect character creation; teach advanced stations through use |
| Weapons | No firearm or ammunition | Make avoidance, tools and conversation relevant |
| Property | No fleet, starter workshop, furnished homestead, stocked chests or machines | Distinguish the campaign from sandbox abundance |
| Safety | A reachable shelter, safe drinking-water source and an NPC who can offer work or food | Bad early choices must not permanently strand the player |

**Opening route target:** shelter within 5–10 minutes, first repair within 15–25, first short drive within 30–45, and a first personally chosen improvement within 60–90. These are playtest targets, not forced timers. Tune quantities to actual consumption, carrying capacity and travel distances.

The stranded car has one readable fault: a missing battery connection and an empty fuel tank, not six hidden failures. Its brakes, tyres and essential fluids are safe for the first trip. A neighbouring wreck provides repair material; the shelter keeper offers a small fuel allowance for restoring a rain collector. The car can be earned through repair, negotiated work, or left behind while the hero travels with a trader. All routes converge at the first settlement.

Once moving, ordinary wear and survival rules apply. Introduce hunger, weather and maintenance one at a time through visible cues. Do not use forced infection, random repeated starting failures or starvation as the first lesson. A broke player can always take local labour, salvage ordinary material or walk to essential services. Recovery costs time or modest debt, not a campaign restart.

## 3. People and opposing interests

| Character | Public face and private pressure | Role across the campaign |
|---|---|---|
| **Mara Vale** | Convoy leader who always brought stranded people aboard; once accepted a corrupt contract to keep that convoy fed | Missing person and emotional anchor; accountable ally, never an infallible quest oracle |
| **Nell Mercer** | Retired roadside mechanic; dry humour, sore hands, excellent memory; refuses to promise what she cannot fix | Opening refuge and eventual neighbour; teaches through paid work rather than lectures |
| **June “Switch” Bell** | WasteTalk relay technician who jokes when frightened; has been editing out distress calls under pressure | Signal investigator, possible travelling companion, potential whistleblower |
| **Ada Venn** | Fuel Guild route superintendent who kept several towns alive through a catastrophic shortage | Principal antagonist; believes central rationing justifies falsifying records and coercion |
| **Sera Dune** | Salt Nomad water surveyor; expects every map to leave someone out | Independent-route advocate; insists that small settlements count too |
| **Brother Cask** | Church of the Last Engine keeper; enthusiastic about engines, quietly doubtful about relic ownership | Connects craft, belief and the existing Last Engine quest without turning faith into a joke |
| **Dr. Ivo Rusk** | Bunker Remnant archivist who preserves records better than relationships | Verifies evidence and old infrastructure; must learn that publishing names can endanger living people |
| **Len Pike** | Young driver hired to impersonate the dead hero on freight manifests; desperate rather than mastermind | Early apparent enemy, later witness or rival; a chance for mercy with practical consequences |

The Fuel Guild is not uniformly evil. Some drivers smuggle medicine through their own checkpoints. Some independent traders exploit shortages. Settlers disagree about accepting displaced convoy workers. The Remnants can authenticate records but are tempted to hoard the machinery. A faction relationship should open approaches without making every member share one personality.

## 4. Main campaign structure and pacing

Three **parallel main storylines** open in the first act. Each has its own characters, problem and satisfying conclusion; they meet in a shared finale. Their order is flexible after the opening.

| Arc | Player's question | Primary play | Persistent payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Dead Air** | Where is Mara, and why was our convoy erased? | Investigation, travel, salvage, infiltration, rescue | Truth, survivors and the ability to broadcast evidence |
| **B · A Place at the Bend** | Can this ruined stop become a home people choose? | Repair, building, farming, power, hauling, defence or negotiation | A working refuge whose services reflect actual investment |
| **C · The Price of Passage** | Can people travel without surrendering their livelihood? | Trade, route surveying, machinery, diplomacy, convoy driving | Alternative supply routes and agreements that shape the ending |

**Proposed duration:** 16–22 hours for the three main arcs and finale at a normal exploratory pace; 10–15 additional hours across the optional quest catalogue. Validate these estimates with real routes and resource costs. Campaign advancement should not require clearing every marker or grinding all factions to Allied.

**Dependency graph:** opening A1 → A2 opens B1 and C1; A3–A6, B1–B5 and C1–C5 can then interleave. The finale unlocks after A6, B3 and C3. B4–B5 and C4–C5 improve preparation and consequences, but remain available before or after the finale. No branch secretly requires an optional relic, a submarine, flight mastery or a high combat skill.

At each main completion, pay the player now and place the next lead in the journal immediately. Give two or three meaningful leads, with estimated distance, known dangers and useful equipment. Optional detours should enrich a journey, not obscure where the story went.

## 5. Arc A — Dead Air

### A1. Someone Left the Radio On — opening

**Hook:** From beneath the wreck, the hero hears Mara counting convoy vehicles. She stops at their callsign. The broadcast ends with “Keep the light on.”

**Play:** Escape through a loose panel, collect personal belongings, reach Nell's roadside shelter and restore its water collector. Diagnose and repair the nearby car or earn a seat on a passing trader's vehicle. A short return to the wreck lets the player recover a convoy enamel badge from an accessible, safe location.

**Choice:** Spend the first payment on a full canteen and food, or on more fuel; Nell explains both routes and guarantees access to water. The badge is optional and later becomes a dashboard keepsake, never a required key.

**Payoff and hook:** Ownership of the repaired car, or a travel voucher plus equivalent repair credit for a later car. Nell recognises the broadcast: Mara's transmitter was supposedly destroyed. Someone has been using it recently.

### A2. The Dead Don't Buy Diesel — first town

**Hook:** A clerk refuses the hero's delivery chit: someone bearing their identity already collected payment, then was recorded dead.

**Play:** Follow three short leads around one settlement: inspect a fuel receipt, question a cook who served the impostor, and trace tyre marks to a repair stall. Any two establish the next destination; the third reveals a useful shortcut or cache. This is a compact investigation, not three distant errands.

**Approaches:** Persuade the clerk, repay a small disputed charge, or recover their dropped ledger from a flooded store basement. Combat is unnecessary. Find Len Pike, who expected an angry debt collector rather than a dead person.

**Choice and consequence:** Len can become a paid witness, be turned over to the local leader, or flee after a threat. His testimony saves effort later, but the stamped receipt remains sufficient evidence. No essential information exists only in his living dialogue.

**Payoff:** Scrap, a service kit and the forged manifest. It bears Ada Venn's office stamp. June's radio callsign and a vacant workshop deed open arcs A3 and B1; Sera's disputed freight claim opens C1.

### A3. A Voice with Yesterday's Weather

**Hook:** June insists Mara is alive because the message is new. Its weather report describes a storm that passed several days ago.

**Play:** Reach a damaged relay, repair its power supply and compare three recorded transmission fragments. Players can climb safely, use a crane platform, or pay a maintenance worker to retrieve the rooftop module. The journal records timestamps; no audio-only puzzle or real-time tuning dexterity is required.

**Reveal:** The distress call is a looping recording, deliberately delayed and retransmitted from different masts. Somebody wants people chasing the voice away from a specific freight route. June admits cutting calls from that route at Guild request.

**Choice:** Publicly accuse June or let her help make the correction. Her trust changes, but the investigation continues through the recording log.

**Payoff:** A repaired receiver, a mast map and access to June's signal-reading assistance. The final fragment contains a live background sound: a lift bell used at an old pumping depot.

### A4. The Weight of Empty Trucks

**Hook:** The convoy's “destroyed” trucks are hauling full loads into a depot that officially receives nothing.

**Play:** Observe a delivery, inspect abandoned cargo seals and get inside. Options include legitimate haul work, forged paperwork built from collected evidence, a service tunnel, or a direct fight. Each route reaches the same physical loading area and reveals actual stored cargo.

**Reveal:** Ada diverted pumps, filters and mechanics to a protected central facility, then erased the transport records. Mara agreed to one secret delivery in exchange for medicine. When she tried to take the workers home, Ada detained the convoy and declared the route lost. Len supplied a disposable identity trail; the hero was presumed killed in the crash.

**Choice:** Take scarce medical supplies for the refuge now or leave a traced shipment intact to expose its recipients. Both obtain documentary proof; one improves immediate care, the other strengthens later testimony.

**Payoff:** A meaningful medical cache or trade payout, warehouse access information and proof that several convoy members are alive. The story answers its initial conspiracy question here, not at the ending.

### A5. No One Rides in the Back

**Hook:** A survivor's message gives a transfer location, not a countdown: “They move us whenever the next tanker arrives.”

**Play:** Prepare seats, water and medical supplies, then explicitly trigger the transfer operation. Buy the workers' contracts, exchange repair labour and evidence, sneak them out, or stop the escort. An owned bus is useful but never mandatory: hire transport or make two safe trips from a secured staging point.

**Complication:** Mara refuses to leave without an injured driver she once abandoned on that secret delivery. This is her reckoning, not a surprise escort speed penalty. The player can stabilise the driver, call local medical transport, or arrange a guarded overnight shelter.

**Choice:** Rescue everyone immediately at greater logistical cost, or extract one group and arrange the rest's release through an agreement. Clearly disclose risks. Do not kill unseen survivors because the player spent ten minutes arranging seats.

**Payoff:** Mara returns as a flawed ally; survivors choose destinations and do not automatically become free labour. One gives the hero a repaired convoy horn. Mara has the original allocation schedule: the coming ration shutdown is a policy decision, not an unavoidable technical failure.

### A6. Tell It Straight

**Hook:** The records can expose Ada, but publishing them unedited identifies the drivers and families who helped smuggle supplies.

**Play:** Bring physical records to Ivo, cross-check one settlement's deliveries, and prepare a broadcast with June. Deliver verification by road or send a trusted courier; neither should require repetitive travel back to every previous site.

**Choice:** Release the full records quickly, redact endangered people while keeping quantities verifiable, or hold the evidence for negotiation. Full disclosure supplies names as well as proof; careful redaction takes an additional corroboration task; private leverage preserves secrecy but can be lost if the agreement fails.

**Payoff:** A playable broadcast rehearsal with captions and a written summary. The personal rescue arc closes: Mara apologises without demanding forgiveness, and the hero decides whether she may stay, travel with them, or depart. The next action is deciding how to reopen the supply network, not finding another hidden mastermind.

## 6. Arc B — A Place at the Bend

The workshop is a candidate home, not the only permitted base. Campaign services attach to a chosen claim with equivalent functional requirements. If a player moves, residents and quest markers migrate through an explicit relocation interaction.

| Quest | Play, decision and character beat | Reward and visible consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **B1 · The Sign Still Stands** | Nell offers the derelict garage in exchange for clearing an unsafe entrance and restoring one usable work area. Shore up a beam, remove it with machinery, or work around the damaged bay. A faded wall records years of customers' repair debts. | Claim/lease, a usable bench and secure storage. The hero paints or keeps the sign. No furnished mansion appears for free. |
| **B2 · Supper for Four** | Supply safe water and prepare a meal for Nell, a passing driver and a displaced gardener. Grow, fish, hunt or buy ingredients; the goal is a functioning kitchen and a shared table, not one mandatory recipe. Ask whom the place should welcome. | Cooking materials, practical Farming/Survival instruction and a recurring visitor. Their seat and the meal become a quiet evening scene. |
| **B3 · Lights Worth Coming Back To** | Wire modest power to a lamp and essential service; provide a roof, bed and sanitation. Choose fuel generation, solar/storage or a viable local renewable source. Test a short outage and restore the priority load. | The base becomes a campaign refuge: an explicit recovery point, a basic service arrangement and a lit landmark. This is the minimum home milestone for the finale. |
| **B4 · Who Gets a Key?** | Survivors and strangers ask for space. Negotiate duties and privacy, build or repurpose rooms, or fund lodging in town. A resident objects to housing a former raider; investigate the actual grievance and set rules instead of taking a universal good/evil option. | Choose one staffed service initially: repair labour, tending crops or medical resupply. Staff consume resources and retain needs; other services remain earnable. |
| **B5 · The Night We Stayed** | A gang demands protection payments after hearing of the refuge. Arrange safe passage, pay a disclosed temporary levy, expose their leader's double-dealing or prepare a defence. The confrontation starts when the player says preparations are ready. | A defended or negotiated home, a shared meal and a wall of visitors' keepsakes. Recurring safety follows the agreement/defences, not permanent invulnerability. |

**Independent conclusion:** The hero chooses the garage's charter: private home with guest space, a paid roadside workshop, or a cooperative refuge. Each has different visitors, costs and services. None must generate effortless passive wealth. A comfortable private home is a valid resolution, not a lesser moral ending.

## 7. Arc C — The Price of Passage

This arc gives long drives and construction a political purpose. Ada's centralized system genuinely keeps some large settlements supplied; simply destroying it would hurt people. Sera argues for redundancy and local agreements rather than replacing one ruler with the player.

| Quest | Play, decision and complication | Reward and next question |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 · Water Has No Flag** | Resolve a detained Nomad water shipment. Compare quantity and water quality against the toll claim, repair a leaking tanker valve, then negotiate or take a legal alternative route. Theft/combat remains possible and has local consequences. | Canteen upgrade or water container, Nomad introduction and an accurate route map. Why do toll records list settlements that receive no deliveries? |
| **C2 · The Cheap Road** | Survey two routes between a village and market. One is short but muddy and controlled; the other longer but public. Drive a small trial load and record fuel, damage and time. Bribe access, improve a crossing or establish a shared hauling agreement. | Tyres matched to the chosen route, route service access and a modest trade contract. Show costs so “cheap” has a practical meaning. |
| **C3 · Enough to Go Around** | Supply three nearby customers from a finite shipment: clinic, farm and public generator. Inspect actual needs; repair a wasteful consumer or trade for a substitute to avoid a simplistic sacrifice. Offer a transparent allocation or favour one customer openly. | Two settlements sign a provisional passage agreement; actual stock/services change. The hero has a workable alternative to total Guild dependence. |
| **C4 · A Bridge You Can Afford** | Restore a safe crossing or shore transfer appropriate to the seed. Use earthwork, a constructed deck, or a ferry/boat transfer; borrow/rent machinery with an explicit return condition. Prepare materials and then test it with a loaded vehicle. | An enduring usable connection, machinery rental privileges and Construction/Driving practice. A road-work sign credits those who helped. |
| **C5 · No Empty Seat** | Organise a demonstration convoy: secure drivers, load agreed cargo, arrange one disputed checkpoint and choose the route. Breakdowns and one road dispute are readable authored events; allies handle ordinary formation instead of needing constant babysitting. | An independent supply agreement, a cargo trailer or comparable service credit, and recurring traffic on the reopened route. Ada must now bargain with a functioning alternative. |

**Independent conclusion:** The route can become a Guild concession with public terms, a local cooperative, or several smaller bilateral agreements. Evaluate resilience and upkeep rather than labelling one universally best. Personal faction standing affects trust and prices, but essential talks remain accessible through evidence, material commitments or a mediator.

## 8. Finale — The Long Way Home

**Setup:** Ada invites the hero to the central pumping and dispatch facility. She offers restored identities, compensation and secure supply for the hero's chosen town if the records remain private. Other settlements will stay rationed. She does not pretend the falsification was an accident: “I had eight running trucks and twelve towns. You found my ugly answer. Show me a working one.”

The player arrives with the actual tools of their campaign: authenticated evidence from A, a refuge/service point from B, and provisional independent distribution from C. Better preparation changes available support, not enemy health scaling. Mara may testify, June may broadcast, Nell may run repairs and Sera may coordinate drivers, according to earlier decisions.

### Three operations, one commitment

1. **Keep people supplied.** Assign available fuel, transport and repair support to a small set of visible destinations. The journal explains which commitments can actually be met.
2. **Take control of the decision.** Negotiate published allocation terms, stage a coordinated work stoppage with an alternative route, or seize the dispatch yard. A combat path must still protect working pumps and transport; destruction alone cannot solve distribution.
3. **Make the run.** Personally deliver the first critical load while allies handle other agreed jobs. Choose a repaired road, a risky shortcut or a prepared water route. The car built over the campaign remains viable; no mandatory war rig or legendary engine.

Before commitment, display consequences and create a recoverable save. The sequence has checkpointed failures: lost cargo can be recovered or replaced, injured allies can be treated, and a failed negotiation opens another approach. No twenty-hour save should become unwinnable because a trailer detached or an NPC wandered away.

### Ending states

| Resolution | What improves | What remains difficult | Playable aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open Roads** | Evidence is corroborated and multiple settlements share allocation authority | Meetings, maintenance and uneven local capacity remain real work | Independent convoys, notice-board shortages and voluntary repair contracts |
| **A Better Bargain** | Guild infrastructure remains, with enforceable published quotas and local oversight | Ada or her successor retains leverage; remote settlements need continued representation | Reliable main routes, audits and disputes over the agreed terms |
| **Break the Locks** | The coercive depot regime is removed by force or sabotage of its control systems | Lost infrastructure and fractured trust reduce near-term supply; people need rebuilding help | Salvage, repair and reconciliation work; no magical prosperity after the boss falls |

Each resolution has stronger or weaker variants based on transport capacity, protected witnesses, local agreements and civilian losses. A well-supported negotiated outcome should be as complete as a militant one. Publish clear consequences, not a secret morality score. The hero can decline Ada's offer, leave preparation unfinished and continue free play; the finale waits.

**Last scene:** At the garage or chosen home, the receiver plays a new message. It is neither a fake nor a call for another world-saving job. A driver asks whether the light means they can stop for the night. The player chooses whether to answer. Then normal play resumes.

## 9. Parallel optional epic — The Last Engine

The existing Last Engine is a supercharged V12 assembled from four relics: bunker block, airfield heads, a road-gang boss's crankshaft and the Church's blowers. Preserve that identity and the current sandbox quest. This campaign proposes richer quest staging, alternative acquisition and a clearer destination; these are additions, not descriptions of existing behaviour.

- **L1 · The Sound Before the Storm:** Brother Cask plays an old engine recording. A veteran recognises the workshop where it was built. Learn why drivers turned an ambitious machine into a legend.
- **L2 · Iron Pilgrimage:** Recover the bunker block using exploration and safe lifting; obtain airfield heads through hangar repair work, barter or salvage. Include a complete small story at each site rather than four identical chests.
- **L3 · A Crankshaft and a Grudge:** Challenge the gang boss through a race, trade a valuable replacement, negotiate with a wronged mechanic or fight. These noncombat alternatives require implementation; the current boss-drop mechanic alone does not supply them.
- **L4 · What a Relic Is For:** Gain the Church's trust through existing standing or a substantial workshop service. Cask must decide whether preserving the engine untouched honours its makers more than letting it run.
- **L5 · The Last Run:** Assemble and tune the V12, then make a marked expedition to a named remote landmark. Reconcile the existing “edge of the world” wording with the planet's east–west wrap; do not send players towards a nonexistent boundary.

**Reward:** The actual engine, its demanding fuel/maintenance trade-offs, a special horn/paint keepsake and a place in radio folklore. It is a prestige machine, not a magical power source or a requirement for the campaign finale. The original first car can carry its own legacy without mounting this engine.

## 10. Standalone NPC side quests

These quests do **not** secretly lead back to Ada, Mara or the central conspiracy. Most can appear in sandbox as well as story mode. A quest should still make sense if the player ignores the main campaign entirely. Named roles are bound to suitable procedural settlements; quest titles and personalities stay authored.

**Reward rules:** Scrap amounts scale with expected labour and consumables. “Training” means a bounded award to an existing skill or a lesson/book implemented for that skill, not an automatic full level. Offer a choice of useful kit or fair-value credit if the player already owns the reward. Named objects are proposed content and need catalogue/art entries. Normal skill use continues to earn ordinary practice.

### S01 · A Fridge Full of Flowers — repair and remembrance
**Giver:** Orla Finch, a flower seller whose cold cabinet now warms everything inside.

**Experience:** Diagnose blocked ventilation and a failed electrical connection, retrieve a replacement from an appliance wreck, then test temperature/power behaviour. Orla is preserving flowers for a partner who died years ago, but decides to sell this year's first bunch instead. Accept or refuse a keepsake without affecting payment.

**Rewards:** Scrap, insulated food storage if that planned system exists (otherwise a storage chest and food), Mechanics training, flower-planter decoration. Cold storage is an implementation dependency, not assumed current functionality.

### S02 · The Goat Has a Lawyer — tracking and mediation
**Giver:** Bess Rill, whose prized goat eats a neighbour's laundry.

**Experience:** Track the escaped goat by damaged plants, lure it with feed and repair the broken enclosure. Decide who pays after discovering the neighbour dismantled a fence rail for firewood. No compulsory animal killing.

**Rewards:** Milk/cheese, feed, Farming practice and a husbandry lesson; a later friendly argument changes according to the settlement.

### S03 · Hearse Power — careful driving
**Giver:** Undertaker Sol Moss, whose hearse has seized on a hill.

**Experience:** Tow it down without overturning the flower trailer, repair or substitute transport, and deliver it to a quiet ceremony. There is no ambush. Smooth arrival earns a modest tip; a rough trip is recoverable, not an instant failure.

**Rewards:** Tow coupler or repair credit, Driving/Mechanics training and a black-and-cream paint scheme.

### S04 · The House That Walked — surveying and construction
**Giver:** Retired mason Della Shaw, convinced her kitchen moved overnight.

**Experience:** Inspect slope erosion and foundation failure, unload furniture, shore the building and move a vulnerable wall or water drain. Repair quality is tested by usable access and support, not merely placing three arbitrary pieces.

**Rewards:** Foundation materials, Construction training and Della's compact porch blueprint.

### S05 · Not That Kind of Shot — marksmanship without murder
**Giver:** Groundskeeper Amos Bell, who runs a neglected range.

**Experience:** Restore targets and complete a safe accuracy course with loaned ammunition, including clearing a jam. An accessible assisted-training alternative offers the lesson without a medal; no PvP or mandatory headshots.

**Rewards:** Firearms practice, a cleaning kit and modest ammo; the precision medal is decorative.

### S06 · Mud, Sweat and Gears — machinery and land stewardship
**Giver:** Farmer Jo Kettle, whose irrigation trench is buried.

**Experience:** Rent/borrow an excavator, clear the channel, load useful soil and deposit it in a marked garden area. Keep a dry access path and protect the neighbour's water intake; careless work creates a repair task.

**Rewards:** Diesel reimbursement, soil/fertiliser, Construction/Farming training and a future machinery-rental discount.

### S07 · An Honest Fish — investigation and fishing
**Giver:** Angler Milt Crowe, accused of buying his competition catch.

**Experience:** Read habitat clues, catch a suitable local species and help run a small weigh-in. Discover that the organiser changed the measuring scale, then expose it publicly or resolve the dispute quietly. Waiting hours for rare RNG is not required.

**Rewards:** A good rod, bait and Survival training; a fish-shaped kitchen ornament and changed local banter.

### S08 · The Wedding at the Wrong End of the Road — social logistics
**Giver:** Tailor Fen Locke, hired to deliver clothes and musicians to two similarly named villages.

**Experience:** Ask for landmarks, resolve the address mix-up, mend a torn outfit and arrange passenger seats. Start the ceremony when the player confirms everyone is ready; no hidden timer during preparation.

**Rewards:** Mended formal clothing with ordinary stats, cloth, Crafting/Speech instruction and a commissioned radio dedication.

### S09 · The Smallest War — farming and observation
**Giver:** Gardener Una Pritch, at war with crows and her own scarecrow.

**Experience:** Observe when crops are damaged, distinguish birds from irrigation stress, and reposition deterrents or repair the water line. Killing every bird is neither necessary nor a permanent solution.

**Rewards:** Seeds for the local climate, Farming training and an unusual scarecrow design; harvest output visibly improves.

### S10 · Twelve Volts of Fame — tuning and fair competition
**Giver:** Teenaged mechanic Pip Harlow, who wants a tiny car to finish a local hill trial.

**Experience:** Inspect tyres, weight, gearing and fluids, then choose a viable route or tune for the measured slope. Completion matters more than winning. Optional best-time medal never gates the practical reward.

**Rewards:** Appropriate tyres, Driving/Mechanics training and a hand-painted number decal. No permanent global speed bonus.

### S11 · Letters Nobody Stole — exploration and privacy
**Giver:** Postal caretaker Reva Dunn, who kept an undelivered bag for twenty years.

**Experience:** Deliver three letters along one useful route. One recipient moved, one refuses the letter and one address is now an orchard. Respect refusals; a neighbour can establish closure without opening private mail.

**Rewards:** Scrap, a small travel bag, Speech practice and three short human endings. None contains a conspiracy code.

### S12 · The Dog at Platform Three — animal trust
**Giver:** Cook Etta Pike, feeding a dog that waits at an abandoned station.

**Experience:** Observe its routine, bring food, remove a paw injury through a proposed animal-treatment interaction, then find its retired owner's new home. Return it, arrange shared care or adopt it only with agreement.

**Rewards:** Survival training, supplies and a dog companion only on the adoption route; other routes receive equivalent practical value. Animal treatment needs a dedicated supported interaction.

### S13 · One Good Roof — rescue and parkour
**Giver:** Roofer Cal Nix, whose partner is stranded above a collapsed stair.

**Experience:** Assess safe access, use vaulting/grapple or build a ladder route, stabilise the casualty and arrange descent. Borrowed access equipment prevents a hard skill gate. Timer starts only if an explicitly accepted storm variant is chosen.

**Rewards:** Athletics/Construction training, rope materials and a roof-repair blueprint.

### S14 · Something in the Well — diagnosis, not a monster hunt
**Giver:** Innkeeper Oona Vale, serving water that tastes of oil.

**Experience:** Trace contamination to a leaking machine upstream, stop the leak, replace a pump filter and supply clean water while the source clears. Inspection and sampled states need implementation; ordinary water graphics alone cannot prove safety.

**Rewards:** Filters, water containers, Mechanics/Survival training and free refills at the inn.

### S15 · The Organ Runs on Diesel — sound and power
**Giver:** Organ builder Hollis Reed, preparing a public recital.

**Experience:** Repair a generator, balance the electrical load and recover missing pipes. Choose a quiet power arrangement or accept the generator noise; the performance reflects that choice. No rhythm minigame is needed.

**Rewards:** Copper coils, Construction/Crafting training, a music recording and a small decorative instrument.

### S16 · Rust in Peace — salvage ethics
**Giver:** Scrap dealer Mo Danner, hired to dismantle a family's abandoned bus.

**Experience:** Inventory usable parts, discover signs of recent habitation and locate the owners. Buy the salvage rights, repair the bus in exchange for spare parts, or knowingly take it and accept the loss of local trust.

**Rewards:** Salvaging practice and a parts bundle proportional to the deal; repair route earns a secure roadside stopping place.

### S17 · Soup Against the Weather — food and supply planning
**Giver:** Kitchen organiser Bea Holt, expecting stranded travellers.

**Experience:** Plan a meal from interchangeable local ingredients, keep fuel available and transport cooked portions safely. Help can be funded through purchases or provided through gathering; preserved-food variants depend on their implementation.

**Rewards:** Food, a cooking lesson in Crafting/Survival and a recipe card; later travellers recognise the kitchen, not the hero as a saviour.

### S18 · A Perfectly Legal Race — route reading and restraint
**Giver:** Bicycle courier Tamsin Rook, challenging motor drivers on a mixed-surface loop.

**Experience:** Scout the course, choose a bike/car and respect occupied pedestrian areas. Shortcuts are legal when physically safe; hitting bystanders disqualifies the medal but still allows restitution and quest closure.

**Rewards:** Driving/Athletics practice, repair supplies and a courier satchel; winning adds cosmetics, not an essential engine.

### S19 · The Bell Beneath the Water — diving and heritage
**Giver:** Boatwright Ester Pell, who wants her village's bell recovered.

**Experience:** Survey a shallow ruin, plan air and ballast, rig the bell and lift it with a boat/crane. Hire a diver or provide shore support as an alternative. Tangled rigging creates a recoverable equipment problem, not a forced drowning scene.

**Rewards:** Diving consumables, Survival/Salvaging training and boat-service credit. The bell becomes an audible landmark in town.

### S20 · Low Clouds, High Hopes — aviation logistics
**Giver:** Surveyor Oren Beck, whose kite-level weather instruments are scattered along a ridge.

**Experience:** Reach sites by aircraft, climbing or a longer ground route. Retrieve instruments and use their readings to advise a local delivery. Flight is a shortcut and lesson opportunity, never the only completion path.

**Rewards:** Fuel, a flight-maintenance booklet granting Mechanics practice, navigation notes and airfield service access.

### S21 · The Last Honest Safe — demolition and restraint
**Giver:** Locksmith Ruth Slate, who has forgotten the combination to her own safe.

**Experience:** Search her old workshop records, repair the lock linkage or prepare a controlled opening. Reckless explosives damage the contents and reduce payment, but still resolve the task. Inside are wage envelopes and an embarrassingly earnest love poem.

**Rewards:** Demolition or Mechanics training according to method, tools and scrap; intact opening adds a useful blueprint.

### S22 · A Jacket for the End of the World — crafting and identity
**Giver:** Tailor Dax Wren, asked to finish a customer's travelling coat.

**Experience:** Source interchangeable cloth/leather, choose insulation versus rain protection, mend it and let the customer test it on a short outing. The customer is leaving a safe home by choice, not being kidnapped.

**Rewards:** Crafting training, sewing supplies and a custom patch for the player's own ordinary clothing.

### S23 · No Teeth, Still Trouble — melee and mercy
**Giver:** Market steward Nia Cole, trying to stop a prizefighter intimidating stallholders.

**Experience:** Gather testimony, arrange a supervised nonlethal bout, expose his rigged debts or negotiate restitution. A bout must have explicit nonlethal rules and a real surrender/stop state; do not assume normal melee damage is safe.

**Rewards:** Melee or Speech training, protective gloves and market credit. A spared opponent may later offer a rematch, not become an instant loyal servant.

### S24 · The Birthday Machine — cheerful salvage puzzle
**Giver:** Grandparent Gus Alder, building a ridiculous moving birthday sculpture.

**Experience:** Find compatible gears, lights and a horn, assemble a safe contraption and choose its final sound. The child likes its wobble more than Gus's planned perfection. Source parts by salvage or trade, with several equivalent combinations.

**Rewards:** Crafting/Salvaging practice, leftover components and a wind-up dashboard ornament. End with a small party, no raider attack.

## 11. Optional side stories that develop over time

These three chains are also unrelated to the central conspiracy. Each has an ending rather than endless escalation. Their later stages open after another activity or a world-day change, with no penalty for returning late.

### P1. Nell's unfinished car — three visits
1. **A second pair of hands:** Help Nell catalogue parts for the car she and her late partner never finished. Learn the value of maintenance over raw power.
2. **The wrong original:** Find that a collector owns the original engine. Pay, barter, or install a practical substitute with Nell's consent. Authenticity is a preference, not the moral answer.
3. **One more Sunday:** Let Nell drive while the hero rides along, or take the wheel if she asks. Stop at a viewpoint for an ordinary conversation.

**Reward:** Mechanics training, access to a precision tool set and a replica paint scheme. The restored car remains Nell's unless she explicitly sells it; emotional closure should not always mean looting someone's keepsake.

### P2. The market that nobody wanted — four jobs
1. Two neighbouring hamlets each insist the other should host a market. Survey water, access and flat ground.
2. Choose a third site or negotiate rotation, then build temporary stalls and sanitation.
3. Mediate a first-day dispute over prices and livestock; use stock records or practical compensation.
4. Run one supply trip and attend opening evening. A small recurring market becomes visible if upkeep is agreed.

**Reward:** Construction/Farming/Speech practice, stall rental income with real costs, or supplier discounts. It must not replace all regional markets with an unlimited vendor beside the player's door.

### P3. The sea does not keep receipts — three expeditions
1. A trawler crew loses its catch to a damaged hold. Diagnose and repair before hunting a supposed sea monster.
2. Survey wreck locations for a cautious salvage expedition; choose shallow safe cargo or deeper valuable equipment.
3. Resolve a salvage-rights dispute between the crew and descendants of the wreck's owners. Pay a share, return personal items or proceed dishonestly with disclosed consequences.

**Reward:** Boat equipment, diving supplies, Salvaging/Survival training and a mooring agreement. A submarine can broaden the expedition but is never required to finish it.

## 12. Rewards, skills and character growth

Use the existing skill set: **Driving, Mechanics, Salvaging, Construction, Crafting, Demolition, Melee, Firearms, Survival, Farming, Speech and Athletics.** Fishing/diving lessons currently map to Survival; cooking/tailoring to Crafting; electrical/mechanical repair to the appropriate Mechanics or Construction activity. Do not invent a new skill tree for every quest.

| Reward tier | Suitable delivery | Balance guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Food, water, filters, bandages, small payments, basic tools | Cover costs and improve resilience without bypassing the first-car arc |
| Middle | Appropriate tyres, quality tools, plans, service access, specialised clothing, modest training | Broaden choices; do not obsolete every crafted item |
| Late | Machinery privileges, difficult blueprints, route agreements, specialist vehicle parts | Attach operating costs and useful trade-offs |
| Personal | Letters, paint, dashboard objects, songs, named landmarks, visitors | Make the world remember actions without giving every keepsake combat stats |

Lessons award one bounded training payment on completion; normal action XP is separate and checked against double-awarding. Never grant both every alternative reward and the chosen item. Failed optional medals do not erase compensation for useful work. Consumable-heavy jobs reimburse expected material use; hired transport and low-skill alternatives must be financially feasible.

Rewards should arrive in a visible handover, a marked parcel or a chosen storage location. Full inventory leaves an accessible claim, not a lost item. Reputation is local and explained: whom the action helped or harmed, not simply “good +10.”

## 13. World integration, presentation and replayability

### Procedural placement

Reserve a safe opening cluster, one viable route between early settlements, an eligible workshop, reachable relay/depot anchors and a late dispatch site when creating a story world. Validate road grades, pedestrian access, water access and climate requirements. Story distance is a pacing budget within the existing 9.6 km planet, not permission to grow the map blindly.

Named anchors decorate suitable existing sites. If a seed cannot support a required scene, choose a validated alternate or deterministically repair placement before play; never silently omit the chapter. Side quests use eligibility tags such as coast, farm, airfield or two connected villages and do not appear where their verbs cannot work. Persist site and NPC identities across unloading and the date line.

### Radio, journal and environmental storytelling

Main scenes have a short voiced exchange, matching captions and a concise journal summary. Keep long exposition optional. Distress calls should never overlap essential driving warnings. Missing a transmission adds a replayable transcript, not a lost quest.

At home, show repaired furniture, changing meal tables, visitors' belongings and evening conversation. On routes, show delivered cargo, working taps and ordinary traffic. Use the existing palette, voxel scale and weather language. A warm porch should remain legible at night without hiding hazards or turning every settlement into a festival.

Radio news reflects verified outcomes and distinguishes rumours from facts. Record whether a story was broadcast, not just whether its quest was completed. Sensitive identities stay redacted if that was the player's choice.

### Failure, freedom and cooperative play

- Essential evidence has redundant recovery: copied records, a witness or an archived dispatch. Murder, fire, lost cargo or streaming must not erase the only continuation.
- Named people can have consequences without requiring blanket immortality. Incapacitation, successors and recorded testimony supply authored alternatives. No unexplained resurrection.
- Main progress survives changing vehicles, moving home, selling nonessential gear and exploring out of order. Finding a site early records discovery and later acknowledges it.
- Optional deadlines display what happens on expiry and lead to a closed or changed quest. They never secretly invalidate the main campaign.
- Propose an authoritative shared campaign ledger for multiplayer: host/world ownership of choices, visible proposal/confirmation for irreversible group decisions, per-player lesson claims and join-in-progress summaries. Current client-local trade/dialogue and host-only taming/riding limitations must be resolved for those story interactions before claiming cooperative campaign support.
- Story saves retain their origin and choices; offer continued free play after the ending. Any story-to-sandbox conversion should be explicit and one-way unless full round-trip support is deliberately built.

## 14. Implementation scope and staged delivery

**Reuse existing foundations:** vehicles and service, survival, production stations, building/utilities, faction standing, dialogue, journal/map, radio, companions, world sites, weather, boats/diving and The Last Engine.

**New work required:** persistent branching quest definitions, campaign start configuration, authored NPC profiles/dialogue, evidence records, world-anchor validation, transactional rewards, scene/escort readiness states, remembered news, campaign-aware recovery and endings. Specialized interactions in side quests—water sampling, temperature-sensitive storage, animal treatment, safe staged bouts and ceremonial performances—need explicit implementation or simpler supported substitutes. They must not be promised by dialogue before they work.

| Stage | Deliverable | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| **N0 · Story contract** | Stable IDs, prerequisite/choice graph, NPC/site binding rules, reward ledger, missing-system register | No circular gate, sole destructible clue or undefined reward; every ending reachable on paper |
| **N1 · First hour** | A1–A2, story start, B1 and three contrasting side quests: S03, S09, S24 | A fresh character reaches first transport and home lead without starvation, forced combat or sandbox grants |
| **N2 · Parallel lives** | A3–A4, B2–B3, C1–C3; a clear mystery reveal and working home/supply effects | Arcs playable in multiple orders; travelling and production produce visible, persistent changes |
| **N3 · Rescue and reckoning** | A5–A6, B4–B5, C4–C5, finale and three aftermaths | Nonviolent and combat completions both work; failed operations recover without losing the campaign |
| **N4 · A world with other stories** | Remaining standalone quests, personal chains and expanded Last Engine staging | Variety/reward matrix complete; unsupported specialist mechanics implemented or replaced honestly |
| **N5 · Delivery pass** | Voices/captions, camera/audio comfort, pacing, multiplayer scope, save migration and unattended regression | Complete representative campaigns and branch tests pass with artifacts; no required manual input |

### Test the story as a playable system

Connect to the roadmap's unattended Q0–Q8 programme. Assign stable quest/step IDs and assert actual state, inventory, world effects and journal output. Automate at least: broke/no-car recovery; low-Speech nonviolent completion; combat completion; all endings; alternative main-arc order; lost evidence; interrupted escort; full-inventory reward; reward claim replay; home relocation; save/reload at every irreversible choice; seam crossing with quest cargo; and cooperative simultaneous claims where supported.

Use fixture saves to cover individual branches efficiently, plus complete no-grant journeys to prove progression. Measure time to first drive, idle/travel fraction, repeated errands, unexpected maintenance stops, time between useful rewards and whether each chapter offers a legible next action. Keep regular optional play observation for humour, attachment and surprise: automation can establish that the campaign functions, but cannot certify that a farewell or a homecoming moves someone.

## 15. What this proposal should leave the player remembering

The first car coughing into life. A name crossed out in a ledger. A warm meal under a repaired roof. A familiar voice finally speaking live. A goat that cost more diplomacy than a checkpoint. A convoy moving because the player made the road usable. And, after all of it, a light left on for the next stranger.

The campaign succeeds when its ending feels like the consequence of how the player travelled, worked and treated people—and when the world still contains worthwhile lives beyond that ending.
